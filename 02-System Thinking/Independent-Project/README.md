# Dana Abu Hassira — Professional Portfolio

> A single-page professional portfolio that presents a UX/UI designer for digital learning, with a non-linear career path, real projects, and hands-on work in AI and automation.

---

## 🎯 Problem

### The Context
I am a UX/UI designer with a Computer Systems Engineering background. My path has not been linear: I worked as a mathematics teacher, a financial literacy trainer, and an online Quran tutor, and I am now training in AI and smart automation.

### The Problem
A traditional CV lists these roles separately and can make the path look scattered. A visitor may not see what connects them or what I can actually do.

### Why It Matters
A recruiter, client or collaborator decides quickly. The portfolio has to show, within moments, who I am, what I design, and how my different experiences support one professional identity.

---

## 💡 Solution

A responsive portfolio website built with **Lovable**, designed as a professional application rather than a CV placed on a page. Its central idea is the headline **"I design digital learning experiences."** and a **My Journey** section that explains how teaching, training and tutoring shaped my design work.

**What does the system do?**

- Introduces who I am and what I do on the first screen.
- Presents two full case studies: **WaitWise** and **Maktabati**.
- Tells the story of my career path in a timeline.
- Shows my AI and automation work, including a Zapier workflow.
- Lists skills, education, training and courses.
- Gives clear ways to get in touch.

---

## 👤 Users

**Primary Users:**
Recruiters, employers, clients and collaborators who want to understand my profile quickly.

**User Need:**
Understand who I am, see real work, and contact me without hunting for information.

---

## 🧠 How I Designed the Solution

Before building, I followed the U2 layers: define the problem, the users, the goals, the requirements, the user flow and the data, then turn them into a specific specification for Lovable.

Main decisions:

- **A single scrolling page** with a fixed navigation, so the whole story is reachable in one place.
- **A narrative for the mixed background.** My Journey connects each role to what it taught me about design.
- **Real data only.** Skills, projects, education and training come from my real profile.
- **Two strong case studies** instead of many weak ones, with smaller work shown separately.

---

## ⚙️ How It Works

```text
Home (identity + "View my work" / "Get in touch")
  ↓
About → Projects (WaitWise, Maktabati) → Case study
  ↓
My Journey → AI & Automation → Skills → Education
  ↓
More Work → Contact
```

**Page sections:** Home, About, Projects, My Journey, AI & Automation, Skills, Education & Training, More Work, Contact.

---

## 🤖 Role of AI

- **Building:** the site was built with Lovable, an AI app builder, from a written specification and refined with targeted instructions.
- **Content:** the **AI & Automation** section presents my own AI and automation work, including a Zapier workflow that extracts follow-up actions from meeting notes with AI and routes them through two paths.
- **Honesty about scope:** this version presents my AI work; it does not include an interactive AI assistant for visitors.

---

## 🗂️ Data

The site is built on fixed profile content:

- **Profile:** name, headline, about, languages.
- **Projects:** case studies (WaitWise, Maktabati) and smaller projects.
- **Experience:** teaching, training and tutoring roles with what each taught me.
- **Skills:** design, tools and professional skills.
- **Education and training:** degree, secondary certificate, Ministry of Finance training and courses.
- **Contact:** email, Behance and LinkedIn.

## 🔑 Key Design Decisions

### Decision 1
**Decision:** Present my path as a story (My Journey) instead of a plain list of jobs.

**Why:** A visitor should see the connection between teaching, training and design, not a scattered CV.

### Decision 2
**Decision:** Show two detailed case studies and keep smaller work in a separate section.

**Why:** Depth in two real projects communicates more than a long list.

### Decision 3
**Decision:** Use only my real information.

**Why:** A portfolio must reflect my true professional identity, and nothing in it should be invented.

---

## 📚 What I Learned

- A good portfolio is a designed product with a user, a goal and a flow, not only a good-looking page.
- The first output of an AI builder is a starting point. Clear, specific instructions and review make it better.
- Defining the problem and the user before writing prompts makes the result more focused.

## 🪞 Short Reflection

**What problem did I design the portfolio to solve?**
Presenting a non-linear path as one clear professional identity.

**Who is the primary user?**
A recruiter, client or collaborator who needs to understand my profile quickly.

**What was the most important design decision?**
Telling the career story through My Journey, so each role supports the UX/UI identity.

**Where did I use AI?**
In building the site with Lovable, and in the work the portfolio presents: AI-assisted educational websites and the Zapier workflow.
